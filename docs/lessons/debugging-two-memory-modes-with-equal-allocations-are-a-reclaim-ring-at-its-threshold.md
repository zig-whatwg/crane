# Debugging: Two steady-memory modes with the same bytes allocated are macOS malloc's reclaim ring at its threshold

**Date**: 2026-11-15
**Lesson**: A perf row that lands in one of two footprint levels, launch by launch, while the process allocates the same bytes in both, is not an engine state: on macOS the system allocator returns freed pages only when its deferred-reclamation ring fills, and a workload whose frees end near the ring's size crosses that threshold in some launches and not in others. `vmmap --summary` names the mode in one line: the `Deferred Reclaim` region's resident size.

**Why**: macOS's allocator queues each freed span in a ring it shares with the kernel and gives the whole ring back when a free finds it full, then doubles it (512 entries at first; lane wrap1's lesson on the ring, `debugging-a-footprint-after-a-burst-of-frees-counts-pages-malloc-queued-for-the-kernel.md`, cites the sources). Everything queued stays in `phys_footprint`. The amount left queued after a burst is therefore a sawtooth of the amount freed, not a monotone function of it. A plain C program shows it with no engine at all (chat2.local, macOS 27.0.1, blocks of 1,500 bytes written, freed, footprint read): 8,000 blocks leave 13.8 of 13.8 MB in the footprint, 12,000 leave 4.2 MB, 16,000 leave 10.5, 20,000 leave 16.8, 30,000 leave 32.6 and 40,000 leave 16.5 - a drain at about 16 MiB freed, the next 32 MiB later. The 16.8 MB stayed for 24 idle seconds, and `malloc_zone_pressure_relief` returned 0 and changed nothing. With `MallocLargeCache=0` in the environment the 20,000-block case leaves 1.5 MB.

**What Happened**: On the perf suite's attributes page, `dom.event-ctor` and the four rows after it read in two levels about 19 MB apart on main itself, about one launch in four low; three cost pairs carried the split as noise or as a gain, and V8's heap was the same in both (lane rep2). Lane mem1 ran 10 launches on chat2.local with `vmmap --summary` at the row's begin mark: 107.8-107.9 MB in 3 launches and 126.7-126.9 MB in 7. In both levels malloc held 90.7-90.8 MB in 115,750 blocks, and V8's and the C++ heap's regions were equal. The `Deferred Reclaim` region was 32K resident in exactly the 3 low launches and 16K in the 7 high ones: in the low launches the ring had filled once, been drained and doubled during the row's warmup (150,000 Events made and dropped); in the high ones it had stopped just short. Malloc's small regions were 30.5 MB dirty against 38.4 MB, and the zone's dirty-but-free column 0 against 7.2 MB. Ten launches with `MallocLargeCache=0` gave one level: 103.7-103.9 MB.

**Fix**:
1. Take `vmmap --summary <pid>` at the mark in several launches (the page is idle while perf_bench answers a mark). Compare the zone's `BYTES ALLOCATED` and the `Deferred Reclaim` line between a low and a high launch: equal bytes with a different ring size is the allocator.
2. Confirm with `MallocLargeCache=0` in the console's environment: the levels become one.
3. Compare two builds on such a row mode against mode (low with low), or with the ring off on both sides; a median over launches compares how often each side crossed the threshold.
4. Do not look for an engine cache or a collection that "sometimes runs": the engine cannot drain the ring, and its allocations are the same in both modes. Chrome, Firefox and Safari do not read this way because they do not use the system allocator for these objects.

**Takeaway**: **Same bytes allocated, two footprints: read `vmmap`'s `Deferred Reclaim` line before theorising. The levels are the reclaim ring before and after a drain, and which one a launch gets is decided by a few frees near the ring's size.**
