# Architecture: A counter moved by frees tells the collector the dead are alive - report a thing gone when it is decided dead

**Date**: 2026-11-14
**Lesson**: A figure an embedder gives a collector must fall when an object is DECIDED dead, before the collector computes its next limit, not when the embedder later frees the memory; a counter that follows allocation and free keeps the dead in the size every new limit is set from, and the limit climbs.

**Why**: V8 sets its next global limit at the end of a full collection from three sizes: its own heap, its C++ heap's marked bytes, and the lowest value the external memory total had since the collection took its sizes (15.7.112.43 src/heap/heap.cc:2595 UpdateConsumedAfterGC, :1604 RecomputeLimits, :6993-6997; heap-controller.cc:309-315). Crane's DOM figure was a counter: up at a node's allocation, down at its free. A dead tree is freed in slices at safe points after the collection that found it dead, so at the limit computation the counter still held that collection's dead trees and the trees earlier collections had queued. Blink has no such figure (its nodes are on V8's C++ heap; V8 reads marked bytes), and WebKit zeroes its extra-memory sum at a full collection and adds only what visited wrappers keep (JavaScriptCore/heap/Heap.cpp:1811-1817, :2254-2270): both give the collector survivors only.

**What Happened**: Lane dm1 traced one script that makes and drops 16,000 trees of 101 nodes (server1, main 929bcfca883). V8's limit went 11.7, 54.8, 104.8, 162.1, 206.9 MB over five collections while 23 to 39 MB was alive; Crane had told V8 39.3, 82.1, 138.0 and 180.2 MB. On the perf suite's parser page V8 was told 70.0 MB at its first limit computation with 14.4 MB alive, and the next limit was 105.7 MB. A measurement prototype that lowered the figure by the bytes of the dying trees at the end of marking (one v8::ExternalMemoryAccounter::Decrease, which never collects: api.cc:12680-12692) and took a tree out of the figure when it was queued held the limit at 47 MB, the script at 114 MB over its start instead of 209, and the parser page's peak at 120.8 MB instead of 156.5. A second defect looked the same and was not: pages of Events and of elements with their attributes collections did not move, because V8 is told 0 B of an Event's 1,510 and 258 B of an element-plus-collection's 950 - too little, not too long.

**Fix**:
1. Keep two numbers on the host: what is held, and what of it is decided dead and not yet freed. Tell the engine the difference.
2. Decide inside the collection for what that collection killed. On V8 the first point where the dead are known and the limit is not yet computed is the C++ heap's weak processing at the end of marking; a decrease there is legal and is in the limit. A prologue callback is too early (nothing is known), an epilogue callback too late.
3. Decide at the release for what dies outside a full collection (a young collection's drop, a removal nobody holds).
4. Read the engine's own trace before and after (--trace-gc-nvp: the limit in force at each collection). A footprint alone shows the peak, the limit shows the cause.
5. Before blaming WHEN a figure moves, measure WHAT it counts per object against what the object holds: a page whose garbage the engine was never told about is not helped by better timing.

**Takeaway**: **A collector's limit is set from what it believes survived. Tell it about a death at the death, inside the collection that caused it - a counter that waits for the free reports garbage as live and ratchets every limit upward.**
