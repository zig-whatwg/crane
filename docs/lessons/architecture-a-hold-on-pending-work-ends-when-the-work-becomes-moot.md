# Architecture: A hold on pending work ends when the work becomes moot, not only when its promise settles

**Date**: 2026-11-03
**Lesson**: A reaction that holds the streams object it will touch (a counted `keepPlatformObjectAlive`) roots that object, and everything it traces, until the reaction ends. A reaction waiting on a promise that never settles never ends, so its hold becomes a permanent root. Give the hold back as soon as the reaction's steps can no longer do anything observable, and keep a liveness check in the steps.

**Why**: Lane streamslife1 made streams wrappers weak and gave every pending reaction a hold on its controller, so that a stream reached only by a pending pull or start is not collected under it. Holds are roots: Blink reaches the same objects through edges from the reaction functions (PipeToEngine's ThenCallables hold a `Member<>`), which die with an unsettleable promise. Crane's holds die only when the reaction runs or is dropped. A reaction is dropped only when its promise is collected, and the held graph usually reaches that promise.

**What Happened**: After the change, every per-call row of streams_lifetime_test went to 0 handles except "pipeThrough read to its end", which kept 15 a call. A TransformStream's readable side pulls on its [[backpressureChangePromise]] (TransformStreamDefaultSourcePullAlgorithm). The last pull, made by the read that sees `done`, gets a new pending backpressure promise. Nothing resolves it once the stream has closed. So the pull reaction held the readable controller, and with it the whole transform, the pipe's streams and their reader, for the realm's life.

**Fix**: `streams_js.ReactionHold`: `Realm.reactHolding` takes an optional keeper, a field in the held object where the hold is parked. A readable controller parks its start and pull reactions' holds (`start_reaction`, `pull_reaction`) and gives them back in ClearAlgorithms (`ReactionHold.giveEarly`). After close, error or cancel, those steps only reset flags on a controller that will never pull again. Each hold is given exactly once, by whichever comes first: the early give or the reaction's end. The steps still check `Hold.alive()` before touching the object, which may by then have been collected. A writable controller's reactions keep their holds to the end: a sink write that settles after the stream errored still rejects a write request script can see.

**Takeaway**: **A hold taken for pending work is a root for exactly as long as the work can matter: give it back when the work becomes moot, not when a promise that may never settle does - and prove each kind with a per-call handle count after collection.**
