# Testing: Nested selector growth needs common-case controls

**Date**: 2026-10-27
**Lesson**: Measure selector-tree growth with both repeated parent references and ordinary nested stylesheets before accepting an ownership representation.

**Why**: Selector representations with identical shallow matching behavior can require very different amounts of storage. Copying an already-resolved parent once per parent reference amplifies every earlier copy, even when every allocation is eventually freed.

**What Happened**: The initial Crane nesting candidate recursively copied the already-resolved parent for every `&`. A counting allocator over std.testing.allocator measured the actual rule parsing and sheet compilation path, with an 8 MiB requested-live-byte budget per case. Between depths 8 and 10, `&&` grew the largest matching AST from 2,299 to 9,211 nodes; mixed `& .x, & .y` grew from 4,339 to 17,395. A 267-byte input retained 2,077,649 bytes at depth 10. All cases freed their allocations, so a leak-only check would pass despite exponential storage growth. The equivalent 200-rule nested sheet retained 1.79 times the flat control and made 1.36 times its allocations.

The resolution shape was informed by WebKit, but the initial ownership citation was wrong: at the pinned revision, CSSSelector's ordinary copy constructor deep-copies rare data, and FixedVector's copy clones its storage. A refcounted RareData type does not by itself establish immutable sharing. See [the actual copy constructor](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/3ef14dfb5202ec892329ebc5e1667dd9d9b88c4c/Source/WebCore/css/CSSSelector.cpp#L875). Q76 corrected the earlier claim before any shared-owner source was applied. The separately checked Stylo revision da29a2a5c1aa17a55182c52a6ee4bc2fb04214b7 does use a shared clone: replace_parent_selector puts the parent clone in Is, and SelectorList's derived clone reaches Arc's count increment through ThinArcUnion. The proposed Crane design was reaffirmed on its measured storage and lifetime grounds.

**Fix**: Before changing the representation, capture a bounded baseline: single `&` at every depth 1–10, repeated `&&` and a mixed parent list at 1/2/4/8/10, and equivalent flat/nested 200-rule sheets. Count AST nodes, allocations, peak/retained live bytes and final-zero cleanup. Stop the expanding representation for the ownership decision, then rerun the same controls after that decision. Keep these untimed storage measurements separate from matcher/parser timing acceptance.

**Result**: The counted immutable parent node is implemented. The unchanged storage regression moved repeated `&&` at depth 20 from an 8 MiB budget failure to 30,386 retained bytes; mixed selectors retain 47,422 bytes. Both free all allocations. From depths 10 to 20 the retained-byte ratios are 1.837 and 1.873, while the rule remains 216 bytes. Exact ownership, copied-holder, guarded-view, immutability and allocation-failure contracts pass. The unchanged 200-rule control retains 289,974 bytes after sharing, down from 353,054 (17.87%); its allocations fall from 7,935 to 6,785 (14.49%). The flat control is byte/allocation-identical at 197,482 bytes and 5,837 allocations. The unchanged five-case lifecycle fixture also passes parent replacement, parent and child deletion, sheet teardown and flat-parent insertion at every allocation failure. These results establish storage and lifetime bounds. Matching speed needs a separate same-mode, alternating comparison on a common base; it cannot be inferred from the smaller representation. Recursive AST visit counters describe logical expansion after sharing and must not be relabeled unique allocated nodes.

**Takeaway**: **Zero leaks does not establish bounded storage growth, and copying an algorithm does not copy its source engine's ownership contract.**
