# Debugging: A TLS handshake that fails only on macOS may be out of descriptors

**Date**: 2026-11-08
**Lesson**: A process inherits macOS's soft descriptor limit of 256, and mbedTLS on macOS opens `/dev/urandom` to seed every TLS connection, so a burst of sockets fails the one wss:// handshake that needs a descriptor after the burst took the last ones.

**Why**: Crane never raised `RLIMIT_NOFILE`, and pool jobs run with `ulimit -n` 256 (hard: unlimited). Each WebSocket holds a curl multi handle (its wakeup pipe, two descriptors) and a socket while it connects. curl seeds a CTR_DRBG per TLS connection (vtls/mbedtls.c), and mbedTLS 3.6's platform entropy is `fopen("/dev/urandom")` on every platform without `getrandom` or `KERN_ARND` (library/entropy_poll.c) - macOS among them. Linux uses `getrandom`, needs no descriptor, and starts processes at 1024.

**What Happened**: websockets/Create-blocked-port.any.js ?wss "Basic check" fired `onerror` in about 60 ms, once in 30 generations and in some A/Bs since October, on macOS hosts only, ?wss only. The file opens its server socket, then 83 sockets to bad ports, which Crane (before lane flaky5's bad-port fix) really connected. A diagnostic build logged curl's error buffer and the process's open descriptors at each handshake's end: the burst peaked at 262 descriptors in a fresh process, and at a soft limit of 240, 230, 210 or 200 the Basic check failed 10/10 with curl 35 `ssl_handshake returned: (-0x0034) CTR_DRBG - The entropy source failed`, fds_open = the limit, its TCP connection already made. At 256, 250 and 220 it passed 10/10: the result is fixed by the exact descriptor count at that moment, so a runner process holding a few more or fewer (kept connections, an earlier file's state) lands on a failing count - the flake. ?default never failed: ws:// needs no new descriptor after its socket. The burst's three "Could not resolve host" errors (lane flaky5 batch 1) were the same limit, reached by getaddrinfo.

**Fix**: The bad ports are refused before any connection (Fetch main fetch step 7; bd6c3de66a0), which took the file's peak to 17 descriptors. And the platform's start raises the soft limit to 8192, or to the hard limit below it, never lowering it or touching the hard limit (`platform.initializePlatform`, kit/posix; 17d8a552a02), as Chromium's `BrowserMainLoop::EarlyInitialization` (`base::IncreaseFdLimitTo(8192)`: "the default limit on Apple is low (256)") and Gecko's `XRE_mainInit` (`mozilla::startup::IncreaseDescriptorLimits`) do.

**Takeaway**: **When a TLS failure is fast, platform-specific and load-dependent, log the process's open descriptors and its `RLIMIT_NOFILE` beside curl's error buffer before theorising: a failure decided by the exact descriptor count is deterministic in a fresh process and a flake in a long-lived one.**
