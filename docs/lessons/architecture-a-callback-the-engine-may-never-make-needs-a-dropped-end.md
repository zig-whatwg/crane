# Architecture: A callback the engine may never make needs a dropped end

**Date**: 2026-10-03
**Lesson**: Host data handed to the engine with a callback - a promise reaction's steps, an asynchronous iterator's steps - is freed by that callback only if it comes. A promise that never settles, or an iterator still alive when its page ends, never calls back, so the data leaked for the life of the process.

**Why**: `engine.PromiseReactionSteps` had `fulfilled` and `rejected` and nothing else. Every host freed its data at the end of those steps. The engine had three other ways to end a reaction: its realm ended first, the collector took the promise with it unsettled, or the promise settled the way no step was given for. None of them told the host. `AsyncIteratorSteps.finalize` ran only when V8 collected the iterator, and an iterator script still held at the page's end is never collected before the agent goes.

**What Happened**: The 0.1 sweeps kept printing 1,166 `leaked:` lines in chunk aak after the first leaks batch reached "zero known leaks" for its sites. Per file, alone: streams/readable-byte-streams/tee 74, readable-streams/async-iterator 82, piping/pipe-through 24, readable-streams/tee 20; fetch request-init-stream, response-stream-disturbed-by-pipe and request-upload.h2 8 each. The traces were all pipeTo's PipeState and Deferreds, tee state, from()'s pull steps and transformToUndefined's holders, each made for a reaction on a promise still pending when the page ended. Three more users kept c_allocator data the same way, invisible to the runner's DebugAllocator: Navigation's Wait, and the window and worker hosts' top-level-await evaluation reactions (one ROOT LEAK under `v8_Promise_React` each in `leaks --atExit`).

**Fix**:
1. The protocol gained a third end. `dropped(data)` runs when the reaction ends without a step. For each `reactToPromise` that succeeded, exactly ONE of `fulfilled`, `rejected` and `dropped` runs, once. The rejected alternative was a `finalize` that runs after the step too. It would have made every host move its free out of its steps, and refcount the data wherever a step re-reacts with it (the pipe loop, from()'s pull). With "exactly one", each host names the free it already had.
2. V8: the two reaction functions share a one-slot holder array as their [[data]], and the slot points at the adapter's record. The record keeps a WEAK handle of the holder; a strong one would keep the realm alive. The weak handle is armed with a second-pass finalizer, so "collected unsettled" runs `dropped` after the collection, never inside it. The record also sits on its realm's list (`realm_finalizers.zig`, on the realm's WrapperCache).
3. The realm's end drains that list at the top of `context_manager.removeContextByKey`, before any of the realm's objects are torn down. For each record it clears the holder's slot, disposes the handle (which cancels a pending second pass), runs `dropped`, and frees the record. Clearing the slot matters: another realm's promise can still hold the functions and settle later, and they must find nothing. Asynchronous iterators use the same record over their state array; after the realm ends, next() and return() reject with a TypeError.
4. tests/v8/realm_end_finalize_test.zig: a never-settled reaction plus a realm end drops once; a settled one runs its step once and never drops; a settle with no step given drops once; a collected one drops once, outside the first pass; an ended realm's reaction on another realm's promise does nothing; no handles or weak records are left. 7 of 9 failed before the fix.

**Takeaway**: **Every callback that carries host data needs an end for each way the engine can fail to call it - settled without a step, collected, realm ended, agent ended - and exactly one end must run. Before freeing, disarm whatever script can still reach, because another realm may still hold the function.**
