# Architecture: A realm's end silences its work before it frees its objects, and frees its global last

**Date**: 2026-10-23
**Lesson**: A worker realm's end freed its objects in its wrapper cache's hash order, global scope among them, and silenced the fetches it left only afterwards; a body freed in that walk ended its fetch, and the fetch reported Resource Timing to a global scope - or a Performance - the same walk had already freed.

**Why**: `worker_realm.destroyWorkerRealmIn` ran `context_manager.removeContext` (the walk: `gc.onObjectFreed` for every wrapped object, in map order) and only then its `retire` step, `WorkerHost.sweepFetches`, which mutes and terminates the fetches whose realm has gone. During the walk every fetch was still live and reporting. A ReadableByteStreamController's teardown released its body pipe, the last reader stopped the transfer (`AsyncFetch.stopProducing`), and the body's end ran the report timing steps (`FetchJob.bodyEnded` -> `reportResourceTimingFor(global)` -> `Performance.performanceOfRealm` -> `dom.global_settings.of`). An unread Response, an XMLHttpRequest, an EventSource and a service worker's relayed body reach the same report from their own teardowns. Whenever the global came earlier in the walk, the report read a freed slot; whenever the global's Performance came earlier, `settingsPerformance` handed back the freed Performance.

**What Happened**: fetch/api/basic/stream-response.any.js [worker] crashed with SIGSEGV in `Instance.stateAs` in 3 of 90 single-process runs for lane wrappercache1, and 0 of 90 for this lane on the same frozen main runner. A temporary instrumented runner, printing the walk's order and each timing report's global generation, found a timing report reading a freed worker global in 40 of 40 runs (1 to 4 per run, in the dedicated and service worker realms), and the freed-Performance variant in 2 of 40. The crash count understated the bug because the slab's free writes its free-list link over `Instance.vtable`: `stateAs` then iterates an `ancestors` slice whose length is the next free slot's stamp - usually 0 (no crash, "no kind owns it", nothing reported), sometimes huge (a fault).

The first fix terminated the worker's fetch group before the walk, as Blink and WebKit stop a context's fetches before they dispose its global. It double-freed at once. With the readers still alive, a body's failure notified them; a closing worker's event loop runs a task's `drop` inside `queueTask` (HTML: a closing worker's queued tasks are discarded), so the EventSource's drop closed the source inside the failure, terminating the fetch being terminated, and released the pipe source that `PipeSource.fail` was still iterating. Blink guards the same moment: `FetchManager::Loader::Dispose` prevents notification before it cancels.

**Fix**: Silence first, free the global last.

1. `WorkerHost.teardownRealm` mutes the agent's fetches at its start (`AgentFetches.stopReporting`: `reporting = false`, nothing called). `AsyncFetch.endBody` reports only while `reporting`, so stopProducing, stopRelay and terminateWith in the walk report nothing. Resource Timing marks entries for the global, and the global is going. The fetch group is still terminated by the post-walk sweep, as before.
2. `context_manager.removeContextByKey` takes a worker's or worklet's global scope out of the wrapper cache before the walk and frees it after, as Blink's `PerformShutdownOnWorkerThread` disposes the global scope after `NotifyContextDestroyed`, and WebKit's `destroyWorkerGlobalScope` releases it after `clearScript`. A guard: Window realms are unchanged.

Red tests: tests/fetch/workerteardown1_stop_reporting_test.zig (behavioural red through a comptime `@hasDecl` guard), tests/v8/workerteardown1_global_last_test.zig (it reads the map's order and adds readers until two follow the global, so it is deterministic), tests/html/workerteardown1_worker_fetch_group_test.zig (the scenario the first fix double-freed).

**Takeaway**: **Before a realm frees its objects, silence the work that reports to it, and free its global last. Terminating that work early is a different change: during a worker's end every notification becomes a synchronous drop callback. And when a freed read usually returns harmless garbage, count the freed reads, not the crashes.**
