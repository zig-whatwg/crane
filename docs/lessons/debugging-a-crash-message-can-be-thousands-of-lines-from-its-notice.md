# Debugging: A crash's message can be thousands of lines from its notice in a 32-runner log

**Date**: 2026-11-14
**Lesson**: In a sweep where 32 runners share one log, a child's panic line and its stack trace are written straight to stderr between the other runners' output. They can land thousands of lines from the supervisor's `!!! crashed on` notice, and can be split from each other. "No message near the notice" does not mean the crash printed nothing.

**Why**: Each child writes its per-file results through a buffer that it flushes after every file. Zig's panic and fault handlers write unbuffered. The supervisor notices the death only after the abort, which can be seconds after the panic line: unwinding and symbolizing the trace takes time, and 31 other runners keep printing meanwhile. The trace's frames therefore arrive in pieces, mixed with other files' results.

**What Happened**: Lane ifr1's FULL A/B on server1 recorded resource-timing/TAO-match.html as CRASH (SIGABRT). The brief and the window's notes both said there was no message. The window's tip.log had `thread 1032963 panic: incorrect alignment` 3,071 lines above `[runner 19] !!! crashed on [10817]`. The stack (worker_thread main -> ThreadHost.endRealm -> unloading_cleanup.run -> fullscreen.unloadingCleanup) came about 1,000 lines below the panic line. The same log had a second crash that no journal blamed: `[runner 27] !!! child 11 ended between files (SIGABRT); no file blamed`, with `General protection exception` at user_activation.windowOfRealm, reached from a service worker's endRealm. Both were the same bug (architecture-a-worker-s-end-must-not-read-the-window-agent-s-state.md), and neither file named by the notices was the cause.

**Fix**: Search the WHOLE log, not the lines around the notice. Run `grep -a -n -E 'panic: |Segmentation fault|General protection|Illegal instruction|Bus error|aborting due' tip.log`. Collect the frames with `grep -a -n -F '(wpt_runner)' tip.log`, plus the `libc.so.6)` frames at the bottom of each trace. Match a panic line to its notice by order and time; the panic carries its thread id. Also grep for `ended between files` and `!!!`, because a crash in teardown blames no file and appears only as a supervisor line. Not done yet, queued: the supervisor could relay each child's stderr through a pipe and prefix every line with `[runner N]`, so that a trace could be extracted by runner.

**Takeaway**: **Before you call a crash silent, grep the whole sweep log for `panic: ` and for the binary's own frames: in a 32-runner log, a crash's message can sit thousands of lines away from its notice.**
