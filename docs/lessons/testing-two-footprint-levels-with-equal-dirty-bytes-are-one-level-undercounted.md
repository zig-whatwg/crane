# Testing: Two footprint levels with equal dirty bytes are one level, undercounted - macOS does not count a reusable page that is written again

**Date**: 2026-11-16
**Lesson**: When a row's footprint reads two levels and `vmmap` shows the same dirty bytes in both, nothing is held in one and released in the other: the lower level leaves out pages that were given up with `MADV_FREE_REUSABLE` and then written again without `MADV_FREE_REUSE`.

**Why**: perf_bench reads macOS's `phys_footprint`. `madvise(MADV_FREE_REUSABLE)` takes pages out of the footprint at once and leaves them mapped. A later write to such a page raises no fault while the kernel has not reclaimed it, so the kernel never learns it is in use again: the page is dirty, resident and uncounted until its owner calls `madvise(MADV_FREE_REUSE)`. An allocator that discards free pages the first way and hands them out again without the second makes its process read low by however many of its live bytes sit in such pages.

**What Happened**: Perf round 9 read `fetch.loopback-gzip` at 99.8 MB in five launches of seven and 135.8 MB in two. Lane mem2 reproduced it on chat2.local with main b0eb9116cea, the fetch page twenty times a build: ReleaseSafe 100-103 MB in 14 launches and 139-141 MB in 6, ReleaseFast 100-102 MB in 14 and 136-139 MB in 6. With libmalloc's deferred reclamation off (`MallocLargeCache=0`) the two levels stayed, 78-82 MB and 115-120 MB, so it was not the ring of the event-constructor rows. `vmmap -summary` at the row's settled mark, six launches: every region's dirty bytes summed to 119.1-119.3 MB in the four high launches (footprint 115.4-115.6 MB) and to 120.4-120.9 MB in the two low ones (footprint 77.6-77.9 MB). The bytes are the row's dead stream chunks - 39 bodies of 1 MiB, each chunk an ArrayBuffer V8 has not collected yet (its trace shows no collection between the row's begin and settled marks in either kind of launch). V8 15.7.112.43 allocates them with its default in-sandbox allocator (`src/sandbox/sandbox.cc`, `DefaultInSandboxAllocator`: 1 MiB chunks). Its `Free` unmaps from the end of its area when the free run reaches the end, and otherwise discards each chunk that became entirely free (`DiscardSystemPages`: `MADV_FREE_REUSABLE` on macOS); its `Allocate` hands a region out with a `memset` and no recommit. The full `vmmap` listing shows which a launch got: 38 dirty 1 MiB regions without a tag in a high launch (unmapped and mapped again: counted), 33 dirty 1 MiB regions that still carry V8's tag in a low one (discarded and reused: not counted). A probe with no V8 in it (lane mem2's `reusable_probe.py`, macOS 27.0.1) gives the rule: 32 MiB written, footprint +32.0 MB; `MADV_FREE_REUSABLE`, +0.0; written again, +0.0; `MADV_FREE_REUSE`, +32.0.

**Fix**:
1. For a row with two footprint levels, read `vmmap -summary` at the mark in a launch of each level before looking for what holds the gap. Equal dirty totals mean the levels are an accounting difference; the higher one is the bytes the process holds.
2. Then find the regions that are dirty in both and counted in one (`vmmap` without `-summary`: the same address range, a different tag or a different count), and read how their allocator gives pages up and takes them back.
3. Turn `MallocLargeCache=0` on as well: it rules libmalloc's ring in or out in the same run, and the two causes can be present together.
4. An allocator of Crane's own that discards pages on macOS calls `MADV_FREE_REUSE` before it reuses them (`src/platform/kit/posix` does, for the Browser's heap). V8's default in-sandbox allocator does not; V8 names it a non-production allocator and gives the embedder `v8::V8::SetInSandboxAllocator` to replace it.

**Takeaway**: **A lower footprint is not less memory until the dirty bytes are lower too: compare `vmmap`'s dirty totals across the two levels first, and suspect `MADV_FREE_REUSABLE` without `MADV_FREE_REUSE` when they are equal.**
